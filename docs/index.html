Likelihood-Free Generative Policy Optimization

Maojiang Su1, Qijie Zhu1, Shuyang Yu1, Yu Xiao1, Minshuo Chen1, Zhaoran Wang1, Han Liu1
1Northwestern University
NeurIPS 2026
Paper arXiv Code

TL;DR: LFGPO lets PPO and GRPO fine-tune diffusion and flow policies without ever evaluating an action likelihood: learn the likelihood ratio with a small network, then distill it into the policy with Doob's drift matching.

Abstract

Diffusion and flow policies have become promising tools for robotic and sequential decision-making systems. Many stable and effective policy optimization methods require evaluating the likelihood or likelihood ratio of an action under the current policy. However, these quantities are generally intractable for diffusion and flow models, which prevents widely used policy update algorithms from being applied to generative policies. We propose Likelihood-Free Generative Policy Optimization (LFGPO), a framework that avoids exact likelihood evaluation by separating policy improvement from generative model training. In the first stage, LFGPO uses a lightweight neural network to represent the likelihood ratio that carries the policy improvement signal. In the second stage, it distills the ratio into diffusion or flow policies through a weighted score- or velocity-matching objective. Our framework incorporates a large family of Reinforcement Learning (RL) algorithms, including PPO and GRPO, to be applied to diffusion and flow policy optimization. We show that the solution to the weighted score matching objective solves a stochastic optimal control problem. Across MuJoCo continuous-control benchmarks, LFGPO delivers strong and stable improvements over competitive diffusion- and flow-policy baselines, showing a practical route to RL fine-tuning for generative policies.

Method

The problem: no likelihood, no PPO

Trust-region methods such as PPO and GRPO update a policy through the ratio \(r_\theta(o,a)=\pi_\theta(a\mid o)/\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid o)\). A diffusion or flow policy produces an action only after simulating a whole denoising or flow trajectory, so \(\pi_\theta(a\mid o)\) has no cheap closed form and this ratio cannot be computed.

Our idea: stop asking the generative model for a likelihood. Separate learning the policy improvement signal from distilling it into the generative policy.

Stage 1: learn the ratio

We train a lightweight network \(r_\beta(o,a)\) to play the role of the intractable ratio. It is optimized with an ordinary RL objective, for example the clipped PPO objective with \(r_\theta\) replaced by \(r_\beta\). Intuitively, \(r_\beta>1\) up-weights an action relative to the behavior policy and \(r_\beta<1\) down-weights it. A small regularizer keeps \(\mathbb{E}_{a\sim\pi_{\text{old}}}[r_\beta]=1\), as a true ratio would. This stage does not depend on the type of generative policy, so PPO, GRPO, policy mirror descent, and soft actor-critic all fit.

Stage 2: distill the ratio with Doob's drift matching

The target is the ratio-tilted policy \(\pi(a\mid o)\propto r_\beta(o,a)\,\pi_{\text{old}}(a\mid o)\). Doob's \(h\)-transform says that this tilt is realized by adding one drift term to the denoising dynamics:

\[ \mathrm{d}X_t=\Big[f(X_t,t)-\sigma_t^2 s_\phi(X_t,t,o)\;{\color{#3273dc}-\;\sigma_t^2\nabla_x\log h_t(X_t,o)}\Big]\mathrm{d}t+\sigma_t\mathrm{d}W_t, \qquad h_0=r_\beta . \]

Lemma 3.1 (Doob's \(h\)-transform). The blue term is the only change to the pretrained reverse process.

Learning that extra drift directly would need samples from the tilted distribution, which we do not have. We therefore use a conditional drift matching loss, which simply re-weights the usual denoising regression target by the learned ratio:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CDM}}(\theta)= \mathbb{E}\Big[\,r_\beta(o,X_0)\,\big\|\nabla_x\log p^{\phi}_{0|t}(X_t\mid X_0,o)-s_\theta(X_t,t,o)\big\|_2^2\Big]. \]

Theorems 3.1 and 3.2: this tractable loss has the same gradient direction as the ideal drift matching objective, for both diffusion and flow policies.

In other words, Stage 2 is ordinary score matching (or flow matching) in which each action is weighted by how much the ratio model likes it. No likelihood is evaluated anywhere.

Why it works: the smallest possible change to the policy

Among all controlled reverse diffusions that reach the tilted action distribution, drift matching picks the one with minimum control energy (Proposition 3.1):

\[ \inf_{u}\;\tfrac12\,\mathbb{E}\!\int_0^T\|\sigma_t u_t(Y_t,o)\|_2^2\,\mathrm{d}t \quad\text{s.t. the endpoint law of } Y \text{ is the tilted policy.} \]

So LFGPO modifies the pretrained diffusion process as little as possible while realizing the desired policy improvement. This gives a theoretical explanation for its stability and efficiency.

One framework, many algorithms

Two design choices span the family: the generative policy class and the policy update rule. When the update has a closed-form target ratio, Stage 1 is unnecessary and LFGPO reduces to a single re-weighted drift matching step. For PPO and GRPO the ratio is learned.

Experiments

We evaluate LFGPO on 10 OpenAI Gym MuJoCo continuous-control tasks covering locomotion, manipulation, and stabilization. Diffusion policies are trained from scratch for 4M environment steps and flow policies for 5M steps, with the same interaction budget for every method within a setting. All numbers are mean ± std over 3 random seeds.

Diffusion policies

LFGPO-PPO obtains the best results on HalfCheetah, Humanoid, Ant, Hopper, Swimmer, and Walker2d, while LFGPO-GRPO performs best on Reacher, Pusher, and Inverted2Pendulum. For example, LFGPO-PPO improves over DIPO by 22.6% on HalfCheetah, 20.9% on Humanoid, 175.2% on Ant, and 12.2% on Walker2d.

Flow policies

LFGPO-GRPO achieves the best result on 9 out of 10 tasks under the same 5M-step budget. It improves over the strongest baseline ReinFlow by 373.8% on HalfCheetah, 71.7% on Hopper, 213.7% on Swimmer, and 738.0% on Walker2d. LFGPO-PMD is a strong second-best on several tasks, showing that the framework works across different policy-gradient methods.
